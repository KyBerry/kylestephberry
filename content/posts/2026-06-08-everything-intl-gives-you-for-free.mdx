Every codebase I have ever poked through has a `formatDate` util, a `formatCurrency` util,
and a `pluralize` helper someone wrote at 6pm on a Thursday. All three are bugs waiting for
their first non-English user. The browser has shipped correct, locale-aware versions of all
of them for years, and they are collectively about as underused as any API on the platform.

Here is the playground I built to make the differences visible. Flip the locale and watch
what changes:

## The roster

**`Intl.NumberFormat`** does currency, percentages, compact notation ("1.2M"), and units
("12 km/h"). The killer detail is that it knows things you would get wrong: the yen has no
minor unit, Germany swaps the comma and period, and some locales put the currency symbol
after the amount. `style: 'currency', currency: 'EUR'` is the whole API surface you need.

**`Intl.DateTimeFormat`** replaces the usual date library for display purposes. The
`dateStyle` and `timeStyle` presets ("full", "long", "medium", "short") map to what a locale
actually expects instead of a format string you invented. If you are shipping `MM/DD/YYYY`
to the whole world, this is the two-line fix.

**`Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`** produces "yesterday", "in 3 days", "vor 2 Tagen". Everyone
builds this by hand with a chain of if-statements, and everyone's version disagrees about
when "yesterday" becomes "2 days ago". The platform's version has an opinion per locale.

**`Intl.PluralRules`** is the one that ends the `count === 1 ? 'item' : 'items'` pattern.
English has two plural categories. Arabic has six. Polish has rules about numbers ending
in 2 through 4. You cannot ternary your way out of this, and you do not have to.

**`Intl.ListFormat`** turns `['Ana', 'Ben', 'Chika']` into "Ana, Ben, and Chika", including
the locale's stance on the Oxford comma. It is the smallest API in the family and it still
beats `arr.join(', ')` the moment a second language shows up.

**`Intl.Segmenter`** is the sleeper. It splits text by grapheme, word, or sentence, which is
the only correct way to truncate a string that might contain emoji or scripts without
spaces. `str.slice(0, 20)` can cut a family emoji in half. The segmenter never will.

**`Intl.Collator`** sorts strings the way the locale expects, so "ä" lands where a German
or a Swede expects it (they disagree, which is the point).

<Callout type="tip">
  Formatting is also an accessibility concern. If you build an `aria-label` by concatenating a raw
  number into a string, a German screen reader user gets `1,234.56` read as nonsense. Run every
  value through the right formatter before it reaches the DOM, visible or not.
</Callout>

## The habit

Treat locale as data that flows into the component, and hand every human-facing value to an
`Intl` formatter on the way out. Construct formatters once and reuse them (`new
Intl.NumberFormat` is not free), or memoize by locale. That is the entire discipline.

The formatting library you are maintaining is not more correct than the platform. It is just
more yours. Delete it.
