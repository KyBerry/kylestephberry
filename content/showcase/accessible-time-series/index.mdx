Charts often become less usable at the exact moment they become interactive. A tooltip follows the
pointer, but it disappears when the pointer leaves and never existed for a keyboard or screen reader
user in the first place. I wanted this chart to treat inspection as a real control rather than a
hover effect.

The plot is one focusable horizontal slider. Its value is the selected week, so Left and Down move
backward, Right and Up move forward, and Home and End jump to the boundaries. `aria-valuetext`
combines the date, activation rate, and change from the previous week; the visual tooltip renders the
same information and stays on screen after the pointer stops moving. One focus target also avoids
making someone tab through twelve data points.

The chart uses a thin sage line, light horizontal rules, and a guide at the selected point. Color is
not responsible for explaining the current value. The header repeats it in text, and a short summary
says what the series did before anyone starts scrubbing.

The data toggle opens a native table with dates, values, and week-over-week changes, giving comparison
tasks and screen reader navigation a structure they already understand. It complements the chart
instead of relying on an accessible name pasted onto an otherwise opaque SVG.

A slider supports sequential inspection rather than direct keyboard access to every point. For a
twelve-week series that is predictable and compact. A multi-year series would need coarser jumps,
range controls, or a different exploration model before adding hundreds of stops to the same
interaction.
