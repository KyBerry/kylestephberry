Internationalization bugs often hide in plain sight because English happens to be forgiving. A
number can be readable with the wrong separators, a date can look plausible in the wrong order, and
plural text can sound close enough that the product ships with a tiny paper cut in every workflow.
This playground makes those differences visible.

The component uses the platform `Intl` APIs instead of formatting strings by hand: `NumberFormat`
for currency and compact counts, `DateTimeFormat` for the event time, `RelativeTimeFormat` for due
dates, `PluralRules` for label changes, and `ListFormat` for collaborator names. The controls are
small, but they cover the surface area where most product UI tends to cheat.

Arabic is included because right-to-left support changes layout, not just copy. The preview card
sets `dir="rtl"` for the Arabic locale, so alignment, inline ordering, and punctuation flow through
the browser's bidirectional text engine instead of a custom class toggle. That keeps the demo closer
to how a production app should behave.

The most useful i18n habit is to treat locale as data, not decoration. If a component receives a
locale, it should hand formatting to locale-aware APIs and let directionality participate in layout
from the top of the subtree.
