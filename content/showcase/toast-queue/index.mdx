Toasts look like the easiest component in the kit: a div that appears and then disappears.
Every hard part is in the "and then disappears."

The first hard part is the timer. A toast that dismisses itself after five seconds is fine
until the user moves their pointer toward it, at which point taking it away is hostile. This
implementation pauses every countdown while the pointer or keyboard focus is anywhere inside
the stack, and the thin progress bar makes the pause visible instead of mysterious. Hover a
toast and watch the bar stop.

The second hard part is volume. Spam the buttons and you will not get eight overlapping
toasts: the stack caps at three visible, and everything past that waits in a queue that
promotes as slots free up. A "+N queued" readout keeps the overflow honest. Dropping
notifications silently is the worst option, and stacking twelve of them is barely better.

The third hard part is announcement. The container is a labeled region, each toast renders
with `role="status"`, and errors upgrade to `role="alert"`. That maps to polite and assertive
live regions respectively: a saved draft should wait its turn, a failed sync should interrupt.
The colored dot is never the only signal, since each toast also carries a visually hidden
kind label ("Success:", "Error:") so the semantics survive without the color.

Motion is deliberately small. Enter is a 200ms rise with a slight scale, exit is a short fade,
and both collapse to instant swaps under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Notifications are
peripheral by definition, and peripheral vision is exactly where large motion is most
distracting.

The dismiss flow has one subtlety worth copying: dismissing marks the toast as leaving and
lets the exit transition play before removal, and queue promotion happens only after the slot
is actually empty. Removing the element immediately and animating a ghost is the common
shortcut, and it is why so many toast stacks flicker when one dismisses mid-pile.
